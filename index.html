1. The 5 Core Rules of Bluffing Blues

Mindset & Phrasing

Bluffing blues guitar comes down to attitude, rhythm, phrasing, and a few high-leverage tricks. The blues rewards conviction and space far more than speed or flashy scale runs.

  1. Own Two Notes Instead of Twenty: Grab a basic minor pentatonic shape in the song's key, pick two or three notes, and make them speak using bends, subtle vibrato, and plenty of silence between phrases.
  2. Fake Theory with "Call and Response": Play a short, punchy 2- or 3-note "question," wait a beat, then play a slightly different "answer." Repeating a motif with small rhythmic tweaks sounds composed.
  3. Use "Safe" Landing Spots on Chord Changes: When the 12-bar cycle shifts to the IV or V chord, hit a strong root or chord tone on the downbeat, hold it, or slide a half-step into a solid home note.
  4. Drop a Classic Turnaround at the End of the Cycle: Nailing bars 11 and 12 to pull the band back to the top sells instant authority.
  5. Commit to the Groove (Even If You Hit a Clinker): Lock into the shuffle pocket. A "wrong" note played once with confidence sounds like a bold choice; played timidly or repeated in a panic, it sounds like a mistake.
Reality Check: Bluffing gets you through a jam with style, not mastery. Treat these tricks as training wheels while you build real ear training, fretboard knowledge, and rhythm chops.

2. 3-Note Soloing Exercise (Key of A)

Key of A • Position 1

Pull up a 12-bar blues backing track in A. Lock your fretting hand into Position 1 (frets 5–8) using just three notes so your ear and rhythm do all the heavy lifting.

5th Fret, B String (E) The Setup — great starting point for call phrases.
8th Fret, B String (G → A) The Soul — bend up a whole step to match the 10th fret (A).
5th Fret, High E String (A) The Home / Root — your safe landing anchor.
Your 3-Note A Box (solid circles) + Blue Note preview on 8th fret G (dashed blue circle).

4-Step Practice Progression

  1. Bars 1–4 (A7 — Question): Pick 5th fret B → 8th fret B, then stop completely for two beats of silence.
  2. Bars 1–4 (A7 — Answer): Repeat 5th fret B → 8th fret B, then land firmly on 5th fret high E with slight vibrato.
  3. Bars 5–8 (D7 → A7 — The Bend): On the IV chord (D7), push 8th fret B upward until it matches the pitch of the 10th fret (whole-step bend), then release smoothly.
  4. Bars 9–12 (E7 → D7 → A7 → E7 — Rhythm Stabs): Play shorter, punchier bursts using only these three notes, landing cleanly on 5th fret high E as Bar 1 restarts.

3. Classic 2-Bar Turnaround (Key of A)

Key of A • Bars 11 & 12

This descending dyad walkdown sits right inside your 5th-to-8th fret neighborhood on the D and B strings.

B|---8-------7-------6-------5-----|---5-----------------------------|
G|---------------------------------|-------7---6---------------------|
D|---7-------6-------5-------4-----|---------------7---5-------------|
A|---------------------------------|-----------------------7---6-----|
E|---------------------------------|---------------------------------|
   1 & a   2 & a   3 & a   4 & a     1 & a   2 & a   3 & a   4 & a
  • Bar 11 Walkdown: Place your ring finger on 7th fret D and pinky on 8th fret B. Pluck both on Beat 1, then slide down one fret per beat: 8/7 → 7/6 → 6/5 → 5/4.
  • Bar 12 Resolution: Land on 5th fret B, then walk down 7th fret G → 6th fret G → 7th fret D → 5th fret D → 7th fret A → 6th fret A to launch straight back into Bar 1.

5. 3-Note Open-String Solo (Key of E)

Key of E • Open Position

Pull up a 12-bar blues backing track in E. Using the open B string gives you effortless sustain and authentic Texas/Delta grit right at the bottom of the neck.

2nd Fret, G String (A → B) Main bending note — bends up a whole step to match the open B string.
Open B String (B) Rings out freely with zero left-hand effort.
3rd Fret, B String (D) Bluesy flat-7th tension — pull off cleanly to open B.

Practice Progression

  1. Bars 1–4 (E7 — Open String Call & Response): Pick 2nd fret G → open B and let it ring for two beats. Answer with 3rd fret B pulled off to open B.
  2. Bars 5–8 (A7 → E7 — Unison Bend): On A7, bend 2nd fret G upward until its pitch locks in with the open B string. Strike both together for a vocal growl.
  3. Bars 9–12 (B7 → A7 → E7 → B7): Play punchy, syncopated stabs alternating between 3rd fret B and open B.

6. Classic 2-Bar Turnaround (Key of E)

Key of E • Bars 11 & 12

The quintessential Robert Johnson / Eric Clapton turnaround walking chromatically down the G string against a ringing high E drone.

   Bar 11 (E7)                       Bar 12 (B7)
e|---0-------0-------0-------0-----|---0-----------------------------|
B|---------------------------------|---------------------------------|
G|---4-------3-------2-------1-----|---2---1-------------------------|
D|---------------------------------|-----------2-------2-------------|
A|---------------------------------|---------------2-----------------|
E|---------------------------------|-------------------0-------------|
   1 & a   2 & a   3 & a   4 & a     1 & a   2 & a   3 & a   4 & a
  • Swing Count: Count 1-and-a, 2-and-a, 3-and-a, 4-and-a. Pluck the G string on the beat number (1, 2, 3, 4) and snap the open high E string on the off-beat triplet (a).
  • Bar 12 Finish: Walk 2nd fret G → 1st fret G → 2nd fret D (note: E), then grab a B7 chord (x-2-1-2-0-2) or hit the open low E string to end.

Seamless Slide Trick: On the very last triplet (a) of Bar 10, touch 2nd fret G and slide straight up into 4th fret G right on Beat 1 of Bar 11 so there is zero audible gap between your solo and turnaround.

8. Expanding Your Palette: The "Blue Note" (♭5)

4th Note Upgrade

Once your 3-note solos feel comfortable, add the flat 5th ("blue note") as a gritty passing tone. Treat it like a spice—brush through it on your way to a stable note rather than sitting on it.

Key Existing 3-Note Anchor Blue Note (Flat 5th) Best Way to Play It
A Blues 5th & 8th fret B, 5th fret high E 8th fret G string (E♭) Walk 5 → 7 → 8 on G as a quick passing note into 5th or 8th fret B.
E Blues 2nd fret G, open B, 3rd fret B 3rd fret G string (B♭) Slide or hammer 2nd → 3rd fret G straight into the open B string.
Golden Rule of the Blue Note: Never stop dead on the flat 5th unless you want extreme unresolved tension. Slide, hammer-on, or pull-off directly into one of your original three safe notes.
